# trickle gap=1000: the delay p99 against the old pool (issue #3)

Status: SETTLED (2026-11-02), kept trade. No library change.

`enqueue_bench 1 20000 trickle` with `TRICKLE_GAP=1000`: one producer submits a task every 1 us into
4 workers. Intel i9-9980HK, macOS 15, Apple clang 17, `-O3`, one `quiet strict` hold, 3 processes of
2 reps each (6 samples per row). The old pool is `test/threadpool_old.hh`, the frozen `threadpool.hh`
of `e961e44` (the START of the issue). The bench now has an `old ThreadPool (A/A)` row (a second old
pool) and a `from-due p99` column: task start minus the time the task was due, which counts the
producer's lateness.

| i9, gap=1000 | delay p99 (submit to start) | delay median | pool CPU per task | from-due p99 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| old, e961e44 | 33-180 us (typ. 41-85) | 6.8-8.5 us | 7.1-7.6 us | 70-79 ms |
| old again (A/A) | 32-50 us | 6.8-8.0 us | 7.3-7.7 us | 76-81 ms |
| new, HEAD | 63-112 us | 5.7-7.2 us | 1.9-2.0 us | 0.20-0.34 ms |

The A/A spread of the old p99 is 32-50 us, wide enough that the old row alone moves by 1.6x; the
new row is 1.5-2x above it in every sample.

## Cause

The issue's hypothesis holds. The old pool takes a mutex and calls `notify_one` on every enqueue,
which costs the producer more than the 1 us gap, so the producer falls behind its schedule: by the end
of a run that is 20 ms nominal, the last task is submitted 70-80 ms late. The pool is then not
offered a trickle but a steady backlog, which the workers drain without sleeping, so a task's
submit-to-start delay does not include a wake. The new pool's enqueue is cheap, the producer keeps the
schedule (late by 0.2-0.3 ms at p99), items arrive 1 us apart into sleeping workers, and the p99 is
the cost of the wake on this CPU. The old pool at the same offered load completes the run about 4x
slower than the schedule, so its row measures a different, easier workload.

## Verdict

Kept trade. The new p99 is the honest wake latency of a trickle that the old pool could not sustain;
the delay median is the same or better (5.7-7.2 us against 6.8-8.5 us) and the pool uses 3.7x less CPU
per task (1.9-2.0 against 7.1-7.7 us). The cost is the coalesced wake and the sleep as soon as the
queue is empty (`pool_queue.h`: a producer signals only while an unsignaled sleeper exists, and
consumers do not spin before sleeping). That is the design that gives the 1 producer, 8 worker enqueue
row 37 ns against 2000 ns, and the faster future round trip. A poller (`archive/perf/trickle-p99`)
recovers part of the p99 at the price of 0.4-0.6x on the 4 producer, 1 worker row, so it is not taken.

The design rules of the work pool (a wait serves only its own group and a self-wait aborts; one listed
ticket per lane and the pop-then-list relay) are not on this path: the row exercises `ThreadPool`'s
`PoolQueue`, and the work pool's chores see no change.

What would reopen it: a trickle row the old pool sustains (gap of 5 us or more), where the old p99
is a fair comparison. At gap=5000 and 50000 the issue's table has no gap, so those are the rows to
read if a latency target needs a number.
